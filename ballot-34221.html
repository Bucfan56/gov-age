First: confirm which district races are on YOUR ballot

34221 is split. Everyone gets the statewide, U.S. House 16, State Senate 20, county-wide and judicial races. State House (70 or 71), County Commission district, School Board district and Palmetto city races depend on your exact address. Your personal sample ballot and polling place are at votemanatee.gov/sample-ballots (name + date of birth). On Election Day you must vote at your assigned precinct. Bring photo ID with a signature.

How to read this

This report does not tell you who to vote for. It lays out who wrote the checks and what the public record shows, so you can judge against your own three questions: commitment to the country and to this area, foreign ties, and big money that could sway them. A donation is a fact about the donor; it is not proof of what the candidate will do. Filings also miss things — dark-money groups, family money, promises.

Every claim carries a link. Controversies are labelled allegation, established, dismissed or fact-checked. Newspaper editorials are shown separately as opinion. The amber boxes hold what the owner's chosen sources (Owen Shroyer, Alex Jones, Stew Peters) say; that content is reported as they say it and has not been checked against records here.

Florida Amendment 1 - Budget Stabilization Fund (rainy-day fund)

every voter

Raises the cap on the state's reserve fund from 10% to 25% of the prior year's general revenue and requires the Legislature to deposit up to $750 million a year until the fund reaches the cap (a transfer can be suspended in limited cases, e.g. if money was drawn that year, or once every five years by two-thirds vote). Needs 60%.

  • No political committee or donors found; TaxWatch notes no formal campaign.

Cost / fiscal impact
  • Required deposits of $750M a year; current maximum about $5B. Florida TaxWatch estimates the fund could reach the cap around 2049 at 2.4% revenue growth (about $16.4B tied up) and might never reach it at 3% growth. No local-government revenue loss. The Legislature passed it 100-1 (House) and 29-4 (Senate).

Florida Amendment 3 - Homestead exemption increase and limits on local property-tax spending ('Save Our Homes From Excessive Property Taxes')

every voter

Raises the non-school homestead exemption to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028 (inflation-indexed after), lets local governments raise it further, cuts the yearly assessment-growth cap for non-homestead property from 10% to 5% (non-school), makes people who establish Florida residency after Dec 31, 2026 wait five years for the full exemption, and limits what local governments may spend property-tax revenue on. School taxes are excluded. Needs 60%; takes effect Jan 1, 2027.
