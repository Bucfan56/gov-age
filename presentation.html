🔒 Why it stays that way

This isn't an accident of who ran.
It's the design.

Of the six bodies on the page, three have neither a term limit nor a retirement age — and they are the three that write the laws and interpret them.

♾️
No limit at all

House · Senate · Supreme Court
No term limit. No retirement age. You leave when you choose, lose, or die.

VS
⏱️
Mandatory retirement

The Joint Chiefs
Four-year terms and a compulsory retirement age.

The only body in the federal government forced to retire its leaders is also, by a wide margin, the youngest group on the page. That is the whole argument in one comparison.

The direction held. The magnitude was an artifact of who we chose to look at. This is the most important slide here: the finding survived being checked, and it got smaller. Any claim that has never been re-measured should be trusted less than this one.

🧭 The honest version

Age is not the only thing
separating these groups.

1
Era, not just ageThe year someone first ran correlates with PAC share at −0.31 on its own. Older members started when PAC cheques were the only practical way to fund a campaign; small-dollar online fundraising only became viable around 2004.
2
Seniority attracts organised money regardless of ageCommittee chairs raise more from interests because they hold the gavel, not because of when they were born.
3
Careers cover unequal spansA member elected in 1975 has forty years behind their percentage. One elected in 2024 has a single cycle.
4
The oldest member cuts against the storyChuck Grassley, 92, has gone from 38% PAC-funded in 1992 to 24% in 2022. The simple version of this argument does not survive contact with him.

🎯 What it means

What this actually supports —
and what it doesn't.

QIs Congress older than the country?
AYes, and by about two decades at the median. That part is not in dispute.
QDo older members take more organised money?
AOn average yes — about twice the share, not six times.
QDoes that mean they are bought?
ANo. This shows who wrote the cheques. It shows nothing about any vote.
QIs it age, or the era they started in?
ABoth. Neither explanation survives alone.
QWhy does the structure matter more than the ages?
AAges change every election. No term limit and no retirement age do not.
QWhat would actually change it?
ATurnover. It is already happening in the open seats, without any rule changing.
